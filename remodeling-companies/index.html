Planning your remodeling companies website: Turn renovation ideas into qualified conversations

Meet homeowners at the planning stage

Someone collecting ideas for a kitchen may be months away from hiring. Explain the types of remodeling you handle and what happens from the initial consultation through planning and construction.

Show the transformation and its context

With the homeowner’s permission, pair before-and-after photos from real jobs with a description of the original problem and the work completed. Explain whether you take on kitchens, bathrooms, additions, or full-home renovations.

Answer the occupied-home questions

Clients often want to know how their household will live through construction. Describe your actual approach to communication, access, dust control, and scheduling without promising outcomes you cannot guarantee.

Questions about your remodeling companies website

Can we group our portfolio by room?
Yes. Real projects can be grouped by kitchen, bathroom, addition, or another category that reflects your work.
Should the site discuss renovation timelines?
It can explain your planning process and factors that affect timing; specific schedules should be confirmed for each project.
Can customers tell us which rooms they want remodeled?
Yes. The inquiry form can request the room, location, desired scope, and preferred timing.
Can we explain how a kitchen remodel is planned?
Yes. Describe your actual consultation, design, selection, and construction steps so homeowners know how your kitchen projects begin.
How should we discuss living in the home during a remodel?
Explain your real approach to access, communication, work areas, and household disruption. The arrangements depend on the project.
Can we show before-and-after photos of remodeling work?
Yes. With permission, pair your own before-and-after images with the work completed and the original condition of the space.
Can the form ask if a homeowner has design plans?
Yes. You can ask whether plans, measurements, or material selections already exist and what help the homeowner is seeking.
Should kitchen and bathroom remodeling have different pages?
They can, when each is a substantial service. Each page can answer room-specific questions and show relevant completed projects.
Can we explain how homeowners choose finishes and fixtures?
Yes. Outline the selection support your team actually provides and when those choices typically fit into your process.
How can we describe an addition versus an interior remodel?
Explain how your company approaches each scope and what initial details you need, such as the existing space and the intended use of the addition.
